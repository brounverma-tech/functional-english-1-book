# Chapter 3: Modal Verbs

## 3.1 Learning Objectives

- identify modal auxiliary verbs;
- express ability, permission, possibility, advice and obligation;
- make polite requests;
- distinguish close meanings such as must, have to and should.

## 3.2 Meaning and Structure

A **modal verb** is a helping verb that adds the speaker's attitude or purpose to the main verb.

> Subject + modal + base form of verb

- She **can speak** English.
- You **should revise** today.
- They **might arrive** late.

Modal ke baad normally **to**, **-s**, **-ed** ya **-ing** nahi lagta. Exceptions such as **ought to** and semi-modals such as **have to** alag patterns follow karte hain.

## 3.3 Main Modals and Functions

| Modal | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can | ability, informal permission | I can swim. / Can I enter? |
| could | past ability, polite request, possibility | Could you help me? |
| may | formal permission, possibility | May I come in? |
| might | weaker possibility | It might rain. |
| will | future, willingness | I will call you. |
| would | polite request, preference, past habit | Would you sit down? |
| shall | offer, suggestion with I/we | Shall we begin? |
| should | advice, expectation | You should rest. |
| must | strong obligation, logical certainty | You must wear an ID card. |
| ought to | moral advice | We ought to respect others. |

## 3.4 Important Contrasts

### 3.4.1 Can vs May

- **Can I leave?** everyday permission.
- **May I leave?** more formal and polite.

### 3.4.2 Must vs Have To

- **I must finish this essay.** Speaker feels the need.
- **I have to submit it by Friday.** External rule or situation.

In normal conversation both can express obligation, but source of pressure may differ.

### 3.4.3 Must Not vs Do Not Have To

- **You must not copy.** Prohibition; copying is not allowed.
- **You do not have to come early.** No necessity; coming early is optional.

### 3.4.4 Should vs Must

- **You should practise daily.** Advice.
- **You must follow the examination rules.** Strong requirement.

## 3.5 Modals in the Past

> modal + have + past participle (V3)

- **should have studied** - correct action was not done.
- **could have won** - past possibility/ability did not become reality.
- **might have forgotten** - uncertain past possibility.
- **must have left** - strong logical conclusion about the past.

Example: “Ravi is not answering. He **might have gone** to class.”

## 3.6 Polite Functional English

| Situation | Useful expression |
|---|---|
| request | Could you repeat that, please? |
| permission | May I use your pen? |
| offer | Shall I open the window? |
| suggestion | We could discuss it tomorrow. |
| advice | You should speak slowly. |
| preference | I would rather study at home. |

Tone matters. “Send the file” is direct; “Could you please send the file?” is more suitable for professional communication.

## 3.7 Common Mistakes

- ❌ He can speaks English. → ✅ He can speak English.
- ❌ You should to study. → ✅ You should study.
- ❌ May you help me? → ✅ Could you help me?
- ❌ You don't must go. → ✅ You must not go. / You do not have to go.
- ❌ I will can attend. → ✅ I will be able to attend.

## 3.8 Practice

Choose a suitable modal:

1. ___ I borrow your notebook, please?
2. You ___ wear a helmet; it is compulsory.
3. She ___ solve complex problems when she was ten.
4. The sky is cloudy. It ___ rain.
5. You ___ have informed the teacher earlier.

**Possible answers:** 1. May/Can, 2. must, 3. could, 4. might/may, 5. should

## 3.9 Quick Revision

- Modal + V1 is the standard structure.
- Use modals to show attitude, certainty and social purpose.
- **Must not** means prohibited; **do not have to** means optional.
- Polite requests commonly use **could** or **would**.

## 3.10 MCQs

1. Which expresses advice? a) should b) must not c) may d) can
2. “It ___ be true, but I am not certain.” a) must b) might c) shall d) ought
3. Correct sentence: a) He can sings. b) He can to sing. c) He can sing. d) He cans sing.

**Answers:** 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
