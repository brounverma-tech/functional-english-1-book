# Chapter 5: Academic, Email and Essay Writing

## 5.1 Learning Objectives

- follow a clear writing process;
- write formal academic paragraphs;
- compose professional emails;
- plan and develop structured essays;
- edit writing for clarity and correctness.

## 5.2 The Writing Process

Good writing ek hi attempt mein perfect nahi hoti. Effective process:

1. **Understand** the purpose, reader and word limit.
2. **Brainstorm** ideas without judging them.
3. **Organise** ideas in an outline.
4. **Draft** the complete message.
5. **Revise** meaning, order and evidence.
6. **Edit** grammar, spelling and punctuation.
7. **Proofread** the final version.

## 5.3 Academic Writing

Academic writing is formal, clear, organised and evidence-based. It avoids unsupported claims, vague language and unnecessary repetition.

### 5.3.1 Paragraph Structure

A strong paragraph commonly has:

- **Topic sentence:** main idea.
- **Supporting sentences:** explanation, reason, evidence or example.
- **Concluding/linking sentence:** closes the idea or connects forward.

> **Topic:** Regular reading improves communication skills. **Support:** It exposes learners to sentence patterns, vocabulary and different viewpoints. Students also observe how ideas are connected logically. **Conclusion:** Therefore, a daily reading habit supports both language accuracy and confident expression.

### 5.3.2 Unity and Coherence

**Unity** means every sentence supports one central idea. **Coherence** means ideas flow logically. Use transitions such as **firstly, however, for example, therefore, in contrast, finally**.

### 5.3.3 Formal Style

| Informal | More academic |
|---|---|
| a lot of | many / considerable |
| kids | children |
| get better | improve |
| I think | the evidence suggests |
| can't | cannot |

Formal writing simple bhi ho sakti hai. Difficult words use karna academic quality ki guarantee nahi hai.

### 5.3.4 Paraphrasing and Plagiarism

Paraphrasing means source idea ko genuinely new wording and structure mein express karna while crediting the source. Sirf kuch synonyms replace karna enough nahi hai. Direct quotation, paraphrase and borrowed data - sabko appropriate citation chahiye.

## 5.4 Email Writing

### 5.4.1 Professional Email Structure

1. Clear subject line
2. Appropriate greeting
3. Purpose in the opening line
4. Necessary details in short paragraphs
5. Specific request or next step
6. Polite closing
7. Name and relevant identification

### 5.4.2 Model Email

**Subject:** Request for Two-Day Extension on Assignment

Dear Ma'am,

I am writing to request a two-day extension for the English assignment due on 10 September. I have been unwell and could not complete the final revision on time.

I have finished the main draft and can submit the revised assignment by 12 September. I would be grateful if you could allow this extension.

Thank you for your consideration.

Regards,  
Aman Sharma  
BCA Semester I

### 5.4.3 Email Etiquette

- Avoid blank or vague subjects like “Hi”.
- Do not write the whole email in capital letters.
- Keep emojis and abbreviations out of formal emails.
- Check attachment before writing “attached”.
- Use **Reply All** only when everyone needs the response.

## 5.5 Essay Writing

An essay develops one central position through connected paragraphs.

### 5.5.1 Basic Structure

**Introduction**

- opening context;
- topic focus;
- thesis statement.

**Body paragraphs**

- one main point per paragraph;
- explanation and evidence;
- connection to thesis.

**Conclusion**

- restate central idea in fresh words;
- summarise key reasoning;
- end with implication or recommendation.

### 5.5.2 Essay Types

- Descriptive: creates a clear picture.
- Narrative: tells an organised experience.
- Expository: explains a topic objectively.
- Argumentative: supports a position with reasons and evidence.
- Compare and contrast: analyses similarities and differences.

### 5.5.3 Sample Outline

**Topic:** Importance of Communication Skills for Students

- Introduction: communication affects learning and opportunity.
- Body 1: improves classroom participation.
- Body 2: supports teamwork and relationships.
- Body 3: prepares students for interviews and employment.
- Conclusion: communication should be practised across the curriculum.

## 5.6 Common Mistakes

- Writing before understanding the question.
- Mixing several ideas in one paragraph.
- Using long sentences without punctuation.
- Repeating the same point in different words.
- Using chat abbreviations in formal work.
- Ending an essay with a completely new argument.

## 5.7 Editing Checklist

- Is the purpose clear in the first paragraph?
- Does each paragraph have one main idea?
- Are examples relevant?
- Do subject and verb agree?
- Are tense and point of view consistent?
- Are spelling and punctuation correct?
- Is the tone suitable for the reader?

## 5.8 Practice

1. Write a formal email requesting information about a workshop.
2. Write a 150-word paragraph on “The Value of Time.”
3. Prepare an outline for an argumentative essay: “Online Learning: Opportunity or Challenge?”
