# Chapter 14: Idioms and Expressions

## 14.1 Learning Objectives

- understand literal and figurative meaning;
- use common idioms naturally and appropriately;
- select expressions for greetings, requests and conversation;
- avoid forced or incorrect idiom use.

## 14.2 What Is an Idiom?

An **idiom** is a fixed or semi-fixed expression whose overall meaning cannot always be understood from the literal meanings of its individual words.

> “Break the ice” means start a friendly interaction, not physically break frozen water.

## 14.4 Functional Expressions

### 14.4.1 Greetings and Introductions

- Good morning. How are you?
- It is nice to meet you.
- Have we met before?
- May I introduce my classmate, Riya?
- How have you been?

### 14.4.2 Starting and Continuing Conversation

- How is your course going?
- What do you think about this idea?
- That reminds me of...
- Speaking of assignments, have you completed yours?
- Could you tell me more about that?

### 14.4.3 Asking for Clarification

- Sorry, I did not catch that.
- Could you say that again, please?
- What exactly do you mean by...?
- Do you mean that...?
- Could you give an example?

### 14.4.4 Agreeing and Disagreeing Politely

- I completely agree.
- That is a good point.
- I agree to some extent, but...
- I understand your view; however...
- I am not sure I see it that way.

### 14.4.5 Requests and Offers

- Could you please help me with this form?
- Would you mind closing the door?
- Shall I send you the notes?
- Would you like some help?
- Let me know if I can assist.

### 14.4.6 Ending a Conversation

- It was good talking to you.
- I should let you get back to work.
- Let us continue this discussion tomorrow.
- Take care. See you soon.

## 14.5 Formality and Context

Some idioms are suitable for friendly conversation but not for every academic document. “The test was a piece of cake” is natural in casual speech. A formal report may say “The test was relatively easy.”

Before using an idiom, ask:

1. Do I know its exact meaning?
2. Is its grammar fixed?
3. Is the situation formal or informal?
4. Will the listener understand it?

## 14.6 Phrasal Verbs vs Idioms

A **phrasal verb** contains a verb plus particle: **turn down** an offer, **look after** a child. An idiom may be a longer expression: **the tip of the iceberg**. Both may have meanings that are not fully literal.

## 14.7 Common Mistakes

- Mixing two idioms: ❌ “We will cross that bridge under the carpet.”
- Changing a fixed word: ❌ “once in a green moon.”
- Using too many idioms in one paragraph.
- Using an idiom whose tone is inappropriate.
- Translating a Hindi idiom word-for-word into English.

## 14.9 Quick Revision

- Idioms carry figurative meaning.
- Learn the complete expression with context and example.
- Functional expressions make conversation polite and clear.
- Formality determines which expression is suitable.

## 14.10 MCQs

1. “Under the weather” means: a) outdoors b) unwell c) confused d) wet
2. A polite disagreement is: a) You are wrong. b) Nonsense. c) I understand your view; however... d) Be quiet.
3. “Call it a day” means: a) name the date b) stop work for now c) call someone d) celebrate

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-c, 3-b
