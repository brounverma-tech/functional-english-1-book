# Chapter 13: Listening Skills

## 13.1 Learning Objectives

- distinguish hearing from listening;
- understand active and passive listening;
- use strategies before, during and after listening;
- recognise barriers, tone, stress and key words;
- respond appropriately in conversation.

## 13.2 Hearing vs Listening

**Hearing** is the physical reception of sound. **Listening** is the active process of receiving, selecting, interpreting and responding to spoken information.

Simple Hinglish: Sound ka ear tak pahunchna hearing hai. Speaker ka meaning, feeling aur purpose samajhne ki conscious effort listening hai.

## 13.3 The Listening Process

1. **Receiving:** sounds reach the listener.
2. **Attending:** attention selects important information.
3. **Understanding:** words and context are interpreted.
4. **Remembering:** useful information is retained.
5. **Evaluating:** message and evidence are considered.
6. **Responding:** listener gives verbal or non-verbal feedback.

Weakness at any stage can create misunderstanding.

## 13.4 Types of Listening

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Appreciative | enjoyment | listening to music or a story |
| Informational | learning | attending a lecture |
| Critical | evaluating | assessing a debate or advertisement |
| Empathetic | understanding feelings | listening to a worried friend |
| Discriminative | noticing sound differences | identifying stress or tone |

### 13.4.1 Active Listening

Active listeners focus, avoid premature interruption, observe tone, ask relevant questions and summarise what they heard.

Useful responses:

- “If I understood correctly, you mean...”
- “Could you explain the last point?”
- “So the deadline is Friday, right?”
- “That sounds difficult. Would you like to discuss it?”

### 13.4.2 Passive Listening

Passive listening occurs when sound is present but the listener makes little effort to understand or respond. Background audio while doing another task is a common example. It may be fine for entertainment but is unreliable for important instructions.

## 13.5 Barriers to Effective Listening

- physical noise or poor audio;
- mobile notifications and multitasking;
- unfamiliar accent or vocabulary;
- emotional reaction to the speaker;
- assuming the message before it ends;
- planning a reply instead of listening;
- fatigue, stress or lack of interest;
- prejudice about the speaker.

**Solution:** remove distractions, identify purpose, note key words, delay judgment, ask for clarification and confirm important details.

## 13.6 Listening for Different Levels

### 13.6.1 Gist

Main topic aur general purpose identify karo. Every word samajhna necessary nahi.

### 13.6.2 Specific Information

Dates, names, numbers, locations, reasons or instructions listen karo.

### 13.6.3 Inference

Speaker directly kya nahi bol raha but tone/context kya suggest karte hain? Example: “The room is rather warm” may indirectly request opening a window.

### 13.6.4 Attitude and Tone

The same words can express sincerity, irritation, surprise or sarcasm depending on stress and intonation.

## 13.7 Pronunciation Features

- **Word stress:** PREsent (noun) vs preSENT (verb)
- **Sentence stress:** content words normally receive more force.
- **Intonation:** rising, falling or changing pitch.
- **Connected speech:** sounds may link or reduce in natural conversation.
- **Pausing:** divides ideas and signals importance.

Good listener ko har accent imitate nahi karna; clarity aur meaning recognise karna hai.

## 13.8 Note-Taking Strategy

Use a simple three-column format:

| Key point | Supporting detail | Question/action |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment due Friday | submit PDF on portal | confirm word limit |

Write phrases, not full sentences. Use symbols: **→** leads to, **↑** increase, **↓** decrease, **?** unclear, **ex.** example.

## 13.9 Three-Stage Practice

### Before Listening

- predict topic from title;
- recall known vocabulary;
- decide what information is needed.

### During Listening

- first listen for gist;
- second listen for details;
- note stressed or repeated ideas;
- do not stop mentally at one unknown word.

### After Listening

- compare notes with the purpose;
- summarise in your own words;
- check uncertain information;
- reflect on missed sounds or vocabulary.

## 13.10 Practice Script

Ask a partner to read this twice:

> The communication workshop will be held in Room 204 on Thursday at 11 a.m. Participants should bring a notebook and arrive ten minutes early. Registration closes on Tuesday evening.

Answer:

1. Where will the workshop be held?
2. When does it begin?
3. What should participants bring?
4. When does registration close?

**Answers:** Room 204; Thursday at 11 a.m.; a notebook; Tuesday evening.

## 13.11 Common Mistakes

- Trying to translate every word while listening.
- Interrupting before the speaker completes the idea.
- Treating silence as listening while attention is elsewhere.
- Ignoring tone and body language.
- Failing to confirm numbers, names or deadlines.

## 13.12 Quick Revision

Listening is an active cycle of receiving, understanding, evaluating and responding. Purpose-based listening, attention to key words and respectful clarification improve accuracy.

## 13.13 MCQs

1. Listening for the main idea is listening for: a) gist b) spelling c) silence d) grammar only
2. Understanding a friend's feelings requires: a) critical b) empathetic c) passive d) selective hearing
3. Best way to confirm information: a) guess b) interrupt c) paraphrase and ask d) ignore

**Answers:** 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
