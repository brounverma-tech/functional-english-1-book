# Appendix C: Reading Practice and Answer Key

## C.1 Passage

Many students believe that good communication means speaking without pause. In reality, effective communication also depends on preparation and listening. A speaker must organise ideas, select examples for the audience and observe whether the message is understood. A listener must pay attention, ask relevant questions and avoid judging before the speaker finishes. When both sides accept responsibility, even a difficult conversation can become productive.

### Questions

1. What mistaken belief do some students have?
2. Name two responsibilities of a speaker.
3. Name two responsibilities of a listener.
4. Find a word meaning “useful and producing a good result.”
5. Give a suitable title.

### Answers

1. They believe good communication only means speaking continuously.
2. Any two: organising ideas, choosing suitable examples, checking understanding.
3. Any two: paying attention, asking relevant questions, delaying judgment.
4. Productive.
5. Suggested: “Shared Responsibility in Communication.”

## C.2 Reading Lesson Review

1. What makes Grandma Moses a symbol of hope?  
   **Answer:** She adapted to difficulty, began serious painting late and continued creating with optimism.
2. How does Tagore combine freedom and discipline?  
   **Answer:** He followed independent interests but produced work through sustained effort.
3. Give two values of music education.  
   **Answer:** Any two: memory, attention, emotional expression, coordination, social learning, cultural participation.
4. How can film support international relations?  
   **Answer:** It creates familiarity, curiosity and people-to-people cultural understanding.
5. What does the star symbolise in Chapter 10?  
   **Answer:** Purpose, guidance, hope or a deeper value.
6. Why is short fiction powerful?  
   **Answer:** Its concentration can create a strong imaginative and emotional effect in limited space.
7. What is Josh's central error?  
   **Answer:** He converts incomplete information and personal dislike into confident moral judgment.

## C.3 Fact, Inference or Opinion

Label each:

1. The workshop begins at 10 a.m. - **Fact** if stated in the text.
2. The speaker may be nervous because her voice shakes. - **Inference**.
3. Every student should learn music. - **Opinion/claim**.

## C.4 Vocabulary in Context

Choose the closest meaning:

1. “The programme had a **significant** impact.”  
   a) minor b) important c) hidden d) harmful
2. “His answer was **concise**.”  
   a) brief and clear b) angry c) copied d) uncertain
3. “She showed **resilience** after failure.”  
   a) ability to recover b) fear c) wealth d) speed

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-a, 3-a

## C.5 Summary Checklist

- Identify the central idea.
- Select only major supporting points.
- Use your own sentence structure.
- Maintain the original meaning.
- Remove repetition and minor detail.
- Keep the summary objective.
